# Is the "Improved" Mixed Hole-Punching Actually More Realistic?

*Written 2026-06-02 on `fresh/v1.0-restart`. A first-principles critique of realism, comparing
v1.0's one-generator-per-dataset approach against the abandoned arc's per-column / composition
"mixed" hole-punching (branch `experiment/subtype-layer`, inspected via `git show` — NOT merged).*

This is the PI's core open doubt. The abandoned arc's central selling point was that its
**mixed/heterogeneous** missingness — different mechanisms on different columns of one dataset,
tuned to match a "realism footprint" — is *more realistic* than v1.0's blunt "one mechanism per
dataset." This document asks whether that claim survives contact with **how real missingness
actually arises**, and concludes: **the mixed machinery is a genuine improvement on one specific
axis (co-existence of mechanisms within a table) but the broader "more realistic" claim is
overstated, and on the axis that matters most for the dissertation's thesis it changes nothing.**

---

## 1. First principles: how real missingness actually arises

Strip away the modelling and ask what *physically* produces a blank cell in a real survey or
tabular dataset. There are a small number of distinct generative processes, and they do **not**
map cleanly onto MCAR/MAR/MNAR:

1. **Item nonresponse.** A respondent sees a question and declines to answer it. Drivers:
   sensitivity (income, drug use, sexual behaviour → MNAR: the *value* drives the refusal),
   fatigue/length (later items → roughly MCAR-ish in value but structured by position), confusion,
   "prefer not to say." *Mixed within a single respondent and a single column.*

2. **Skip logic / routing.** The instrument *itself* decides not to ask. "Are you employed? If no,
   skip the next 8 job questions." The gate answer is observed, so the absence is **MAR by
   construction — and deterministic, not stochastic.** Whole blocks of columns vanish together for
   a subpopulation defined by an observed answer. This is the dominant structural source in real
   questionnaires and it is *not noise* — it is design.

3. **Planned / matrix-sampled missingness.** PISA, NAEP, ECLS-K hand different respondents
   different *booklets*; nobody answers everything. This is **MCAR by deliberate randomization**,
   but with a column-bimodal, block-aligned footprint utterly unlike Bernoulli noise.

4. **Limit-of-detection / censoring.** An assay or instrument cannot record below a floor (or above
   a ceiling). The value is missing **because of what the value is → MNAR**, with a *sharp* truncated
   observed distribution. (Survey analogue: top-coded income, "≥ \$200k".)

5. **Wave attrition / dropout (longitudinal).** Respondents leave a panel and never return. Missing
   is **monotone** (a clean suffix across waves) and driven by a mix of observed covariates (MAR)
   and unobserved trajectory (MNAR). Strongly *structured*, not per-cell independent.

6. **Unit nonresponse.** An entire respondent (row) is absent or near-empty — refused the survey,
   couldn't be contacted. Often handled by weighting, but in an item-level table it shows up as
   near-empty rows, driven by demographics correlated with contactability.

**The single most important fact for this critique:** real missingness is **(a) heterogeneous
across columns within one table, (b) block-structured and often deterministic (skip logic), (c)
monotone in time (attrition), and (d) a confounded blend of MAR and MNAR within a single column**
(a respondent skips an income question partly because they're a student — MAR on employment — and
partly because they're rich — MNAR on income). No mechanism is "pure," and the analyst can never
recover which it was.

---

## 2. What v1.0 does, and where it is honestly wrong

v1.0 applies **one generator (one mechanism class) to the entire dataset** per training sample. Its
realism deficits, stated plainly:

- **No within-table heterogeneity.** A real survey table is never "all MAR." v1.0's training
  distribution contains no example where column 3 is skip-logic-MAR while column 7 is
  sensitivity-MNAR and column 12 is fatigue-MCAR. This is the genuine, defensible gap the mixed
  arc set out to fill.
- **No deterministic skip logic at scale, no monotone attrition.** v1.0's `SkipLogic`/`Branching`/
  `SectionLevel` approximate routing *stochastically* (a `conditional_rate`), and there is no
  cross-column monotone (suffix) structure. Real routing is deterministic and real attrition is
  monotone.
- **The label is a whole-table fiction.** "This 48-column table is MNAR" is not a statement any
  survey methodologist would make; mechanism is a *per-cell* (at best per-column) property.

So v1.0's realism is genuinely limited. The question is whether the mixed arc *fixes* this in a way
that is real rather than cosmetic.

---

## 3. What the mixed arc actually adds

From the abandoned branch (`lacuna/data/mixed_missingness.py`, `composition_sampler.py`,
`mar_column_pool.py`, `mnar_column_pool.py`, `missingness_footprint.py`, `real_mask_sampler.py`):

1. **Per-column mechanisms in one table** (`mixed_missingness`): each column independently assigned
   OBSERVED / MCAR / MAR / MNAR, masked with the *same* generator classes v1.0 trained on, and a
   **per-column label vector** returned as ground truth. → Directly addresses §2's heterogeneity
   gap. **This is real.**
2. **Composition control** (`composition_*`): plan a *target by-cell composition* (what fraction of
   missing cells are MCAR/MAR/MNAR), realise it with disjoint column/block allocation, and stamp a
   **per-cell mechanism tag**. The estimand is redefined to the *missing cell* with a consistent
   denominator. → A cleaner statistical object than "one label per table."
3. **Block structure** (`composition_blocks`): co-missingness / few-pattern / monotone blocks, so
   MAR and MNAR can carry the column-block footprint that pure per-column independence lacks.
4. **A realism *ruler*** (`missingness_footprint` + `real_mask_sampler`): a 20-feature, scale-free,
   mechanism-blind summary of a mask (column-rate distribution, row patterns, co-missingness,
   pattern-count, monotonicity, MAR-coupling, MNAR-shape), with a sampler that draws analyst-
   plausible blocks from a *real* pooled survey table (e.g. NHANES). The claim: synthetic masks are
   "realistic" iff their footprint distribution is statistically indistinguishable from real
   blocks'. → This is the most intellectually serious part: it makes "realistic" *falsifiable*.
5. **Subtype diversity** (`mnar_column_pool`'s 25 own-value MNAR subtypes, MAR pool variants): a
   per-column mechanism is realised by a *random subtype* at a *matched marginal rate*, so mechanism
   *type* varies while missing *quantity* is held fixed.

---

## 4. The critique: where "more realistic" is overstated

Granting (1)–(5) are real engineering, the realism claim still overreaches on four counts.

### 4a. Per-column *independence* is itself unrealistic in the opposite direction
`mixed_missingness` assigns each column a mechanism **independently** and (in the clean regime)
forces MAR predictors to be drawn only from *fully observed / MCAR* columns. Real surveys are the
opposite: the columns that go missing together are **topically coupled** (a whole "income & assets"
module, a whole "sensitive behaviours" battery), and the predictor of a MAR column is very often
*itself partially missing* (you skip the income-detail question because you skipped the
"do you have income?" question). The clean regime **deliberately excludes** exactly the entangled
case that dominates reality (it is "a later ablation" that was never trusted/finished). So v1.0 is
unrealistic by *over-coupling* (one mechanism for everything); the clean mixed regime is unrealistic
by *over-decoupling* (independent columns, observed-only predictors). Neither is the real
block-correlated, partially-observed-predictor structure.

### 4b. The footprint ruler measures *distributional plausibility*, not *mechanism realism*
`missingness_footprint` can certify that a synthetic mask *looks like* a real mask on 20 observable
statistics. But the entire premise of the project (and of Molenberghs, and the PI's "missing in
real life is missing forever") is that **the mechanism is not identifiable from the observed data.**
A footprint is computed from the mask and the observed values — i.e. from *exactly the information
that cannot distinguish MAR from MNAR.* So matching the footprint of real data proves the *masks*
are plausible; it says **nothing** about whether the *mechanism labels* we stamped are the ones that
really operated. You can match NHANES's footprint perfectly with a mask we *labelled* MNAR when the
true (unknowable) process was MAR. The ruler measures the realism of the *holes*, not the realism of
the *reasons* — and the reasons are what the model is trained to predict. This is the deepest issue:
**a more realistic-looking mask does not make the label more true.**

### 4c. Subtype diversity at matched rate raises a confound, doesn't settle it
The arc's own most important finding (carried in the reset memory) is that **MAR-vs-MNAR separation
on natural-rate data was substantially a *miss-rate* artifact**: at matched marginal rate, MAR vs
MNAR collapses toward chance (AUC ≈ 0.55–0.61) even for the full MoE, because MNAR-on-correlated-
columns leaves a MAR-like coupled footprint (Molenberghs floor). The `mnar_column_pool` *correctly*
holds rate fixed to remove that confound — but doing so reveals that the remaining signal is weak,
which *undercuts* rather than supports a "we made it more realistic and it still works" story.
Realism here makes the task *harder and closer to the identifiability floor*, not more solvable.

### 4d. The hardest real sources are still missing or only stochastically approximated
Even the mixed arc does not generate: **deterministic** skip logic at full instrument scale, true
**monotone wave attrition** as a temporal process, **unit nonresponse** (near-empty rows) as a
first-class mechanism, or the **MAR∧MNAR-within-one-column** blend that is the empirical norm. The
`composition_blocks` monotone option and booklet MCAR are gestures at (1)/(3)/(5) of §1, but the
generative story is still "pick a column, apply a parametric link to (its own | another) z-scored
value." Real routing is a *logical rule over answers*, not a sigmoid.

---

## 5. Verdict

| Realism axis | v1.0 (one-gen-per-table) | Mixed arc (per-column / composition) |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanisms co-exist within a table | ✗ absent | ✓ **genuine gain** |
| Per-cell / per-column estimand | ✗ table-level fiction | ✓ cleaner object |
| Block / co-missingness structure | partial (survey MAR gens) | ✓ explicit blocks |
| Falsifiable "realism" check | ✗ none | ✓ footprint ruler (but see 4b) |
| Topical coupling of missing columns | ✗ | ✗ (clean regime decouples) |
| Partially-observed MAR predictors | n/a | ✗ (excluded by design) |
| Deterministic skip logic at scale | ✗ (stochastic approx) | ✗ (stochastic approx) |
| Monotone attrition / unit nonresponse | ✗ | partial / ✗ |
| Makes the *mechanism label* more true | — | ✗ (4b — unidentifiable) |
| MAR-vs-MNAR solvable at matched rate | ✗ (rate-confound exposed) | ✗ (confound *correctly* removed → weak signal) |

**Bottom line.** The mixed hole-punching is **more realistic in one concrete, defensible sense**:
real tables carry *several mechanisms at once*, and v1.0 cannot represent that. If the dissertation's
claim were "Lacuna handles tables with heterogeneous per-column mechanisms," the mixed machinery
would be the right tool and a real improvement.

But the **global "more realistic" claim is overstated**, for three reasons that compound:
1. It trades v1.0's over-coupling for an equal-and-opposite over-decoupling (4a), still missing the
   *block-correlated, partially-observed-predictor* structure that actually dominates surveys.
2. Its realism ruler validates the **mask's appearance**, which is precisely the information that
   *cannot* validate the **mechanism label** — the thing being predicted (4b). A prettier hole is
   not a truer reason.
3. When it *does* control the rate confound honestly, the MAR-vs-MNAR signal it is trying to make
   "more realistic" collapses to near the identifiability floor (4c) — so added realism buys a
   harder, not a more convincing, result.

This is consistent with — and arguably *explains* — why the PI lost trust in the arc: it invested
heavily in making the *masks* look real while the *labels* (the actual prediction target) remained
fundamentally unidentifiable, and the one rigorous confound control it added (matched rate) quietly
demolished the headline MAR/MNAR separation. v1.0's honesty is cruder but its limitation is at least
*visible* ("one mechanism per table" is obviously a simplification); the mixed arc's limitation is
*disguised* by a realism apparatus that measures the wrong thing.

**Recommendation for the fresh start.** Keep the *one true* defensible realism gain — heterogeneous
per-column mechanisms — as a clearly scoped, separately motivated extension *if and only if* the
thesis needs per-column claims, and build it in v1.0's own tree (Coding Bible Rule 8). Do **not**
re-adopt the footprint-as-realism-proof framing as evidence the *labels* are realistic; it cannot
bear that weight. And treat MCAR-vs-structured (identifiable) as the load-bearing result, with
MAR-vs-MNAR reported honestly at matched rate as near-floor — which is the true, if modest, state of
the science here.
